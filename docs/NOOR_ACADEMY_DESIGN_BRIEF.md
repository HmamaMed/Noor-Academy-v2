# Noor Academy — Brand Identity & Website Redesign Brief

> Upload this file to the ChatGPT project as reference material. Use the prompt at the end to request a design template. Treat product facts as constraints and the proposed identity as a creative starting point.

## 1. The design goal

Create a beautiful, modern, attractive, and simple website for **Noor Academy**. Make the academy feel welcoming, credible, and thoughtfully designed. Visitors should quickly understand what they can learn and how to join a class; returning users should easily find their schedule and class materials.

Start with the **public homepage**, then extend the same identity to course pages and the student experience. The brief covers the whole website so the homepage belongs to a coherent system.

Success means:

- A recognizable identity rather than a generic education template.
- Strong typography, generous space, and a clear visual hierarchy.
- A polished mobile experience, with straightforward navigation and readable content.
- One clear primary action per section or task.
- Enough warmth to attract learners, and enough restraint for everyday study and administration.

## 2. What the product actually does

Noor Academy is an academy management and class materials platform. Classes are organized into scheduled groups with assigned teachers, rooms, and limited capacity.

The owner has confirmed that **Noor Academy is about enlightenment through learning, curiosity, and personal growth across subjects**. Its launch identity is not religious. Islamic courses may be offered in the future as one subject among others.

The repository's historical demonstration catalog includes Quran Memorization, Arabic Language Fundamentals, Islamic Studies, and Tajweed Mastery. These are demonstration records, not confirmation of the academy's launch offering or its positioning. Confirm real course titles with the owner before publishing a launch catalog.

### Audiences and their tasks

| Audience | Main needs |
| --- | --- |
| Visitors | Understand the academy, explore courses, inspect available groups, create an account. |
| Students | Apply to a group, track confirmation, check weekly schedules, access class resources, manage their profile. |
| Teachers | See assigned groups, inspect confirmed student rosters, publish lessons, exercises, and corrections. |
| Administrators | Manage courses, groups, rooms, users, and enrollment decisions. |

### Enrollment flow — preserve this behavior

1. Browse courses and open a course detail page.
2. Review a group's teacher, room, dates, weekly sessions, and availability.
3. Sign in or register, then apply to an eligible active group.
4. The application becomes **pending**. Payment is handled manually outside an online checkout.
5. An administrator confirms enrollment after receiving payment. Pending applications expire after **72 hours** if unconfirmed.
6. Confirmed students access their class workspace and materials, including materials from completed groups.

An application does **not** mean enrollment is confirmed or a seat is guaranteed. Explain the pending state calmly and visibly. Avoid copy such as “Your seat is booked” before confirmation.

### Class workspace

- Materials are organized as lessons, exercises, and corrections.
- Resources can contain text, existing Google Drive/Docs links, and downloadable attachments.
- Private uploads depend on academy storage being configured; reflect availability in the interface.
- Students read and download resources. Teachers and administrators publish and remove them.

### Scope boundaries

The current product does not include online payment checkout, student submissions, grading, chat, or a video streaming platform. Do not add these to the default template.

Course records currently contain a title, description, and syllabus. Group records supply teachers, rooms, dates, schedules, status, and seat availability. Prices, ratings, course cover images, difficulty levels, and learning progress are not existing course fields. Any design using them must identify them as proposed additions or explicit mock content.

## 3. Proposed brand identity

**Confirmed positioning:** A welcoming academy that helps people broaden their understanding and grow through learning. Enlightenment, curiosity, clarity, and personal growth guide the identity across course subjects.

**Working promise:** Clear guidance. Thoughtful learning. Steady growth.

**Personality:** Warm, calm, trustworthy, purposeful, contemporary.

**Voice:** Clear and encouraging. Speak to learners directly. Use short sentences and concrete actions. Avoid exaggerated promises, corporate jargon, and pressure-based enrollment language.

The name “Noor” expresses **light, clarity, and growth**. Express this through spacious layouts, thoughtful typography, and a restrained visual motif. Arabic calligraphy may represent the academy's name without defining a religious focus. Specific logo refinements remain visual proposals until reviewed by the owner.

Use imagery about discovery, ideas, learning, and human development. Keep the main identity broad: avoid religious symbols or sacred text as brand decoration. Future specialist courses can have their own subject-appropriate visuals without changing the academy's overall positioning.

### Example copy direction

- Hero headline: **“A brighter path to learning.”**
- Hero supporting copy: “Explore Noor Academy's courses, choose a class that fits your schedule, and keep your learning materials in one place.”
- Primary CTA: **“Explore courses”**
- Secondary CTA: **“How enrollment works”**
- Course CTA: **“View available groups”**
- Application CTA: **“Apply to this group”**
- Pending status: “Your application is awaiting confirmation. Pending applications expire after 72 hours. Enrollment is confirmed after payment is received and an administrator approves your application.”

These are draft design examples; refine them to match the academy's confirmed positioning.

## 4. Recommended visual direction

**Direction: warm light, deep green, and quiet elegance.**

Use an ivory canvas, deep green anchors, restrained brass accents, and clean typography. Balance a welcoming editorial homepage with practical, calmer application screens. The existing indigo/yellow palette can be replaced; visual continuity is not a requirement.

### Starting color tokens

| Token | Suggested value | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Page background | `#F7F5EF` | Warm ivory canvas |
| Surface | `#FFFFFF` | Cards, forms, menus |
| Primary | `#163D35` | Main buttons, key headings, navigation accents |
| Primary hover | `#0F2D27` | Hover and pressed states |
| Soft primary | `#E8F0EB` | Selected items and quiet section backgrounds |
| Accent | `#B58A43` | Small highlights and decorative details |
| Main text | `#182924` | Headlines and body text |
| Muted text | `#52635B` | Secondary information |
| Border | `#DCE3DC` | Dividers and input outlines |

These are starting values, not an accessibility-certified palette. Check contrast in each actual pairing. Use accent gold mainly for decoration; default primary buttons to white text on deep green. Give success, warning, and error states separate semantic treatments with visible labels.

### Typography and layout

- Use a clean sans serif such as Inter for body text and application screens, with system fallbacks.
- Consider a restrained serif for large marketing headings, such as an open-source editorial face. Keep tables, forms, and course metadata in the sans serif.
- Limit the system to two font families and a small set of weights.
- Body text should generally be 16–18 px, with comfortable line height. Avoid tiny course metadata.
- Use a consistent spacing scale based on 4/8 px increments, a public-page content width around 1200 px, and generous section spacing.
- Use softly rounded cards, subtle borders, and very light shadows. Reserve large rounded shapes for a few featured elements.
- Design clear primary, secondary, and text-link actions. Keep icons consistent in style and weight.

### Imagery and motion

Use one intentional hero visual: an authentic learning photograph with permission, or a tasteful illustration about learning and light. Provide a polished fallback when imagery is unavailable. Do not invent a real academy location, staff member, or facility through stock or generated imagery.

Keep motion subtle and brief: button feedback, small hover changes, and optional gentle section entrances. Respect reduced-motion preferences. Avoid autoplay video, distracting parallax, large repeated gradients, excessive floating cards, and emoji as the main icon system.

## 5. Homepage template

| Section | Content and design intent |
| --- | --- |
| Header | Noor Academy wordmark, Courses, an in-page enrollment link, Sign in, and one primary Explore courses CTA. Use a compact mobile menu. |
| Hero | Strong headline, short explanation, primary and secondary CTAs, and one purposeful visual. Keep the main message and CTA visible quickly on mobile. |
| Learning benefits | Three concise points grounded in the product: scheduled group classes, teacher-led learning, and shared class resources. |
| Featured courses | A small curated preview using the example subjects. Show title, short description, and View available groups; offer a link to the complete catalog. |
| How enrollment works | Three simple steps: choose a course and group; apply and arrange payment; receive confirmation and access your class. Explain the 72-hour pending window nearby. |
| Learning experience | Show how weekly schedules and organized materials support students. A labeled illustrative workspace preview can communicate this without invented progress statistics. |
| Final invitation | A concise encouragement and Explore courses CTA. |
| Footer | Brand, existing navigation, and clearly labeled placeholders for owner-provided contact or policy details. |

Use varied section composition while keeping the rhythm calm. Avoid turning every section into the same row of cards. Additional About, FAQ, testimonial, or contact sections are optional proposals and need real content or clearly labeled placeholders.

Do not fabricate student counts, reviews, success rates, accreditation, instructor qualifications, partnerships, addresses, prices, or testimonials. Omit unsupported trust claims from a production-ready version.

## 6. Supporting pages and shared components

| Page / route | Design priority |
| --- | --- |
| Homepage `/` | Establish identity and lead visitors to course exploration. |
| Catalog `/courses` | Easy scanning, consistent cards, clear loading and empty states. Search is an optional enhancement; propose filters only when data supports them. |
| Course detail `/courses/:id` | Description and syllabus alongside available groups. Clearly show dates, sessions, teacher, room, and remaining seats. Stack the group panel below the overview on mobile. |
| Authentication `/login`, `/register`, `/forgot-password`, `/reset-password` | Focused forms, visible labels, inline validation, password controls, and clear recovery outcomes. |
| Student `/student/dashboard` | Prioritize upcoming classes and workspace access, followed by pending applications and enrollment history. Explain pending deadlines without alarmist styling. |
| Teacher `/teacher/dashboard` | Assigned groups, schedules, roster access, and clear entry to material publishing. |
| Workspace `/classroom/:groupId` | Readable resources, category filters, downloads, and a publishing form visible only to authorized teachers/admins. |
| Profile `/profile` | Simple account editing with clear save feedback. |
| Admin `/admin/courses`, `/admin/groups`, `/admin/rooms`, `/admin/users`, `/admin/enrollments` | Consistent navigation, readable tables, practical forms, and explicit confirmation/cancellation actions. |

Define reusable navigation, buttons, course cards, group cards, status badges, fields, alerts, tables, resource cards, and empty states. Marketing pages may be more expressive; dashboards should emphasize clarity and efficient work.

## 7. Responsive behavior, accessibility, and states

- Provide desktop and mobile designs, including a realistic layout around 390 px wide.
- Stack hero content and cards naturally; use a compact menu rather than squeezed desktop links.
- On mobile, use schedule lists or day cards instead of compressing a full weekly calendar.
- Keep admin tables usable through responsive layouts or deliberate horizontal scrolling.
- Provide visible keyboard focus, semantic headings, labeled fields, adequate contrast, and touch targets around 44 px.
- Never communicate status through color alone. Pair colors with labels and, where useful, icons.
- Design loading, empty, error, success, disabled, and submitting states.
- Differentiate full groups, inactive groups, and unavailable groups accurately. Show a clear reason when applying is disabled.
- Include expired/cancelled applications, restricted workspace access, unavailable uploads, and download failures.
- Default copy to English, matching the current app. Arabic/French support remains an owner decision. If requested, include proper Arabic typography and RTL layouts, not just translated labels.

## 8. Implementation context

The existing frontend uses **Angular 22**, **TypeScript**, **standalone components**, **reactive forms**, **RxJS**, and **Tailwind CSS 4**. The backend is Spring Boot with PostgreSQL.

If implementation code is requested, provide Angular-compatible templates and Tailwind styling, with reusable components and centralized design tokens. Preserve route names, role permissions, and enrollment behavior. Avoid introducing a different frontend framework or new UI libraries without explaining the need.

For a visual template, use realistic, clearly labeled example content. Distinguish a static mockup from functioning authentication, enrollment, or material downloads. Separate proposed new features from the existing UI redesign.

## 9. Owner decisions to confirm later

These do not need to block a first design concept. State assumptions and proceed.

- Final academy mission and whether the demonstration subjects reflect the live offering.
- Learner age groups and whether parents are part of the intended audience.
- Country, city, languages, and any currency or pricing information.
- Existing logo, approved imagery, and permission to use real learning photos.
- Exact manual payment instructions and public contact details.
- Whether to adopt the proposed green/ivory identity or explore another palette.
